Financial operations platform

Trade breaks don’t need finding.
They need deciding.

Finding breaks is a hundred lines of pandas. Deciding what each one is, what it actually costs, and who should fix it first is a person’s entire morning. Awynn does that first pass — and shows its work.

The problem

Detection is solved.
Triage is not.

Every morning a fund compares its own record of yesterday’s trades against the confirmations its brokers send back. Where the two disagree, that’s a break. Spotting them is trivial.

What follows is not. Each break has to be classified, explained, priced for the exposure genuinely at risk rather than the size of the discrepancy, and routed to whoever can actually resolve it — all before the next settlement cycle closes.

That work is high-volume, deadline-bound, and almost entirely judgment. Today it is absorbed by hand, by the people who can least afford the hours.

How it works

Three decisions, made in order.

  1. 01

    Classify

    What kind of break is this — price, quantity, settlement date, fees, a confirmation that never arrived? The category determines everything downstream.

  2. 02

    Quantify

    How much money is genuinely at risk, as distinct from how large the discrepancy looks. A big number that nets to nothing is not an emergency.

  3. 03

    Route

    Who should fix it, and in what order, with the reasoning attached so the person receiving it can check the work rather than redo it.

Every step is logged. Nothing executes on its own.

Supervision

Built to be checked.

The near-term equilibrium for AI in financial markets is not autonomy. It is bounded autonomy — agents operating as supervised co-pilots and constrained modules inside human decision processes, with approval workflows, audit trails, and monitoring around them.1

That is a design constraint, not a limitation. Awynn is built so every classification carries its reasoning, every judgment is attributable, and a human sits at the decision boundary by default. In compliance terms, the goal is that an audit is a query rather than an excavation.

It matches where regulators already are: in anti-money-laundering work, agents are being deployed on first-level analyst duties precisely so people can concentrate on higher-risk judgment — with the standing caveat that human oversight remains critical.2
